Amendment 1: Raise the budget reserve cap

No. 1 on the ballot. The Legislature placed it there by a joint resolution, a proposal both houses pass by a three-fifths vote.6 It needs 60 percent of the votes cast on this amendment.1

What is this about?

The budget stabilization fund is the state's reserve for a drop in general revenue or for an emergency. General revenue means the state's main tax collections.2 The constitution now says the reserve must keep at least 5 percent of the last completed year's general revenue and may not hold more than 10 percent.3 This amendment would let the state keep up to 25 percent.4 A 2025 House analysis says it would also require a yearly transfer of the lesser of $750 million or the amount needed to reach 25 percent, unless set conditions are met.5

What does your vote mean?

YES

A yes vote raises the reserve cap from 10 percent to 25 percent of general revenue.4 The 2025 analysis says it also requires that yearly transfer unless the suspension rules apply.5

NO

A no vote leaves the current 10 percent cap in place.3 Withdrawals stay limited to revenue shortfalls and to emergencies that general law defines.2

Who supports it, and who opposes it?

Supports it

  • Republican Party of Florida.7
  • Evan Power, the state party chairman, said it prepares Florida for storms, emergencies, and downturns without raising taxes.8

Opposes it

  • Ron DeSantis.9
  • Florida Education Association.10
  • League of Women Voters of Florida.10

What would it cost?

The 2025 House analysis says the effect on the state budget is indeterminate but likely insignificant. Indeterminate means the analysis did not settle on a dollar amount.11

Exact words on your ballot

Proposing an amendment to the State Constitution to increase the amount of funds that may be retained in the budget stabilization fund from 10% to 25% of general revenue collections, require the legislature to transfer the lesser of $750 million or the amount required to reach 25% of the general revenue collections each year unless certain conditions are met, and allow the legislature to withdraw funds for critical state needs.4
