## Measure H, Cedar Hollow parks levy {#contest-measure-h .contest-title data-part="Measures"}

### What is this about?

The measure would replace a parks levy that is set to end, the one adopted in 2020<a id="cite-25"></a>[<sup>1</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-25). It does not add a second levy on top of the 2020 levy that continues<a id="cite-26"></a>[<sup>1–2</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-25).

::: vote-pair

::: vote-box

#### YES

A yes vote replaces the expiring parks levy at the same rate[<sup>2</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-26). The official fiscal note ties that rate to about 1.2 million dollars a year for parks<a id="cite-27"></a>[<sup>3</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-27).

:::

::: vote-box

#### NO

A no vote lets the current parks levy expire after the 2026 tax year<a id="cite-28"></a>[<sup>4</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-28). The code section on the 2020 levy's expiration is the same one cited for current law[<sup>1</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-25).

:::

:::

### Who supports it, and who opposes it?

::: {.pair}

::: card

#### Supports it

The parks board and the library board signed the argument for Measure H<a id="cite-29"></a>[<sup>5</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-29).

:::

::: card

#### Opposes it

A homeowners association and a grocer signed the argument against Measure H<a id="cite-30"></a>[<sup>6</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-30).

:::

:::

### What would it cost?

The Westland fiscal office note, dated August 1, 2026, estimates 1.2 million dollars a year for parks if the levy passes[<sup>3</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-27), <a id="cite-31"></a>[<sup>7</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-31).

::: ballot-words

#### Exact words on your ballot

The official label is Measure H, a parks levy, and it asks whether Cedar Hollow should replace the expiring parks levy<a id="cite-32"></a>[<sup>8</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-32).

:::

:::

### Measure type and qualification

Measure H is a referral<a id="cite-33"></a>[<sup>9</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-33). The Cedar Hollow council sent it to the ballot by Resolution 2026-14 on July 9, 2026[<sup>9</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-33).

### Current law and proposed change

The current parks levy, adopted in 2020, expires after the 2026 tax year[<sup>1</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-25). A yes vote replaces it at the same rate[<sup>2</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-26). A no vote lets it expire[<sup>4</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-28).

### Vote threshold

The measure passes with more than half the votes cast on it<a id="cite-34"></a>[<sup>10</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-34).

### Funding by side

Yes on H reported 20000 dollars in receipts for the quarter that ended September 30, 2026<a id="cite-35"></a>[<sup>11</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-35). No on H reported 18000 dollars in receipts for that same quarter<a id="cite-36"></a>[<sup>12</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-36).

### Official sponsor or committee

The council resolution names Yes on H as the sponsor committee for Measure H<a id="cite-37"></a>[<sup>13</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-37).

### Top funders

Yes on H reported 8000 dollars from the Cedar Hollow Parks Foundation, the largest receipt in that quarter<a id="cite-38"></a>[<sup>14</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-38). No public record found.

## House Bill 18, parks repair reporting {#contest-bill-hb18 .contest-title data-part="Legislation"}

### What is this about?

House Bill 18 would require a city to post its parks repair list each year<a id="cite-39"></a>[<sup>1</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-39).

::: vote-pair

::: vote-box

#### YES

The Parks Workers Guild told the committee the public list would show which repairs are waiting<a id="cite-40"></a>[<sup>2</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-40). The guild said residents cannot see that queue in the current inventory[<sup>2</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-40).

:::

::: vote-box

#### NO

The City Managers Association told the committee the posting repeats the inventory the city already keeps<a id="cite-41"></a>[<sup>3</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-41). The association said the extra page would not change which repair is done first[<sup>3</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-41).

### What is the history of this bill?

#### Legislative history

House Bill 7 of 2024 required a parks inventory and did not require a public posting<a id="cite-44"></a>[<sup>6</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-44). The 2026 text of House Bill 18 adds a yearly public posting that the 2024 bill did not include<a id="cite-45"></a>[<sup>7</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-45).

#### Status timeline

The House passed House Bill 18 on April 2, 2026, and the bill is in the Senate<a id="cite-46"></a>[<sup>8</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-46).

#### Committee referrals and amendments

The Local Government Committee heard House Bill 18 on March 12, 2026<a id="cite-47"></a>[<sup>9</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-47). The committee adopted an amendment that sets the posting date as June 1<a id="cite-48"></a>[<sup>10</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-48).

#### Companion bills

Senate Bill 22 is the companion to House Bill 18<a id="cite-49"></a>[<sup>11</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-49).

#### Section-by-section changes

Section 12 of the parks code would add a yearly public posting, and that change is written to take effect on July 1, 2027<a id="cite-50"></a>[<sup>12</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-50).

#### Effective date

House Bill 18 takes effect on July 1, 2027<a id="cite-51"></a>[<sup>13</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-51).

#### Current law and proposed change

The city code, in force since 2020, requires a parks inventory and does not require a public posting<a id="cite-52"></a>[<sup>14</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-52). The bill would add that posting[<sup>1</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-39).

### Who sponsored it, and who drafted it?

#### Sponsors and cosponsors

Morgan Hale, a Democrat from House district 4, is the primary sponsor of House Bill 18, and Riley Chen, a Republican from House district 9, is the cosponsor<a id="cite-53"></a>[<sup>15</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-53).

#### Drafting and model legislation

Legislative counsel drafted the posted text of House Bill 18<a id="cite-54"></a>[<sup>16</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-54). A Westland Ledger article said the posting lines resemble a union model bill<a id="cite-55"></a>[<sup>17</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-55). The article is that paper's comparison, not a finding that the legislature copied a text[<sup>17</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-55).

#### Implementing agency

The bill names the Parks Division as the implementing agency<a id="cite-56"></a>[<sup>18</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-56).

### Who lobbied or testified?

#### Interest groups and lobbying

The Parks Workers Guild registered to lobby for House Bill 18<a id="cite-57"></a>[<sup>19</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-57). The City Managers Association registered to lobby against House Bill 18<a id="cite-58"></a>[<sup>20</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-58).

#### Hearing testimony

A guild witness told the committee the posting would show delayed repairs<a id="cite-59"></a>[<sup>21</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-59).

#### Sponsor contributions

The Parks Workers Guild gave Morgan Hale 500 dollars in 2024<a id="cite-60"></a>[<sup>22</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-60).

### How did the chambers vote?

#### Roll-call votes

The House vote on April 2, 2026 was 28 yes and 12 no<a id="cite-61"></a>[<sup>23</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-61).

#### Party vote counts

On that roll call, Democrats cast 18 yes and 2 no, and Republicans cast 10 yes and 10 no. Those totals are the journal lines for each party<a id="cite-62"></a>[<sup>24</sup>](guide-sources.md#source-62).

### How did your legislators vote?
