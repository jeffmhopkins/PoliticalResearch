## Measure H, Cedar Hollow parks levy {#contest-measure-h .contest-title}

### What is this about?

The measure would replace a parks levy that is set to end, the one adopted in 2020<a id="cite-30"></a>[<sup>30</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-30). It does not add a second levy on top of the 2020 levy that continues[<sup>30</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-30), <a id="cite-31"></a>[<sup>31</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-31).

::: vote-pair

::: vote-box

#### YES

A yes vote replaces the expiring parks levy at the same rate[<sup>31</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-31). The official fiscal note ties that rate to about 1.2 million dollars a year for parks<a id="cite-32"></a>[<sup>32</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-32).

:::

::: vote-box

A no vote lets the current parks levy expire after the 2026 tax year<a id="cite-33"></a>[<sup>33</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-33). The code section on the 2020 levy's expiration is the same one cited for current law[<sup>30</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-30).

### What is the history of this bill?

#### Legislative history

House Bill 7 of 2024 required a parks inventory and did not require a public posting<a id="cite-47"></a>[<sup>47</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-47). The 2026 text of House Bill 18 adds a yearly public posting that the 2024 bill did not include<a id="cite-48"></a>[<sup>48</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-48).

#### Status timeline

The House passed House Bill 18 on April 2, 2026, and the bill is in the Senate<a id="cite-49"></a>[<sup>49</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-49).

#### Committee referrals and amendments

The Local Government Committee heard House Bill 18 on March 12, 2026<a id="cite-50"></a>[<sup>50</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-50). The committee adopted an amendment that sets the posting date as June 1<a id="cite-51"></a>[<sup>51</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-51).

#### Companion bills

Senate Bill 22 is the companion to House Bill 18<a id="cite-52"></a>[<sup>52</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-52).

#### Section-by-section changes

Section 12 of the parks code would add a yearly public posting, and that change is written to take effect on July 1, 2027<a id="cite-53"></a>[<sup>53</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-53).

#### Effective date

House Bill 18 takes effect on July 1, 2027<a id="cite-54"></a>[<sup>54</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-54).

#### Current law and proposed change

The city code, in force since 2020, requires a parks inventory and does not require a public posting<a id="cite-55"></a>[<sup>55</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-55). The bill would add that posting[<sup>4</sup>](guide-full-sources.md#source-4).

# Glossary {#glossary .back-matter}

Levy
: A levy is a tax or charge put on a ballot for voters to accept or reject.

Nonpartisan race
: A nonpartisan race does not list a party on the ballot. A public voter-registration party, when one is cited, is labeled registered party (race is nonpartisan).

Cash on hand
: Cash on hand is the money a campaign reported still having at the end of a filing period.

Filing period
: A filing period is the date range a finance report covers.

Tier T1
: Tier T1 is a primary or official source.

Tier T2
: Tier T2 is an established nonpartisan reference.

Tier T3
: Tier T3 is named journalism.

Tier T4
: Tier T4 is an advocacy source and is quoted only as that group's view.

Single source
: A single source means the sentence rests on one record.

Allegation
: An allegation is a claim someone has made that this guide has not confirmed.

Not found
: Not found means the listed places were searched and the fact was not in them.
